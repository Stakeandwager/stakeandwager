# Participation Policy

**Stakeandwager · Version 1.0 — September 2026**

This sits alongside the Protocol. The Protocol says how a table works. This says
who may sit at one, and what will never be asked of them.

---

## 1. Adults only

**Stakeandwager is for adults. Nobody under 18 may open a table, make a
commitment, or be named in a record.**

A table asks a person to put something real behind a claim and be publicly
associated with the outcome. That is not a reasonable thing to ask of a child,
and no protective wording makes it one.

Where age is unclear, the table does not proceed.

Nothing on Stakeandwager is directed at children, marketed to children, or
designed to appeal to them.

---

## 2. What is never collected

The registry needs very little, and takes nothing beyond it.

**Never asked for:** date of birth, home address, government identification,
financial account details, photographs of people, health information, or
anything about a participant's family.

**A contributor gives:** a name or handle of their choosing, and an email
address so the result can reach them. Nothing else is required, and nothing else
should be volunteered.

A handle is sufficient. Nobody is required to use a legal name to participate.

---

## 3. Third parties

**A stake may only be made of what the staker controls.**

You may not stake another person's time, labour, property, or reputation. You
may not name a third party in a table as a resource, a guarantor, or a reason to
believe the claim — unless that person has themselves opened or joined the table.

This is both a protocol rule and a safeguarding one. It prevents a record being
built about somebody who never agreed to be in it.

---

## 4. Publication and consent

**Published by default:** the table — creator, stake, proposition, milestone,
deadline, verification tier — and its resolution.

**Counted, not named:** participation. How many committed, how many completed.

**Named only with permission:** individual contributors. Consent is asked at the
time of contribution, not assumed and not requested later.

**Never published:** the content of what a contributor wrote, unless they
release it. Their assessment is working material, not a public record.

A participant may withdraw their name from the published record at any time. The
count remains; the name goes.

---

## 5. No profiling

The registry records what happened at tables. It does not build pictures of
people.

**It will not:** infer traits, score reliability, rank participants, sell or
share participant data, or use records to assess anyone for a purpose outside
the table they joined.

Section 10 of the Protocol states this as a design rule. This states it as a
commitment to the person.

---

## 6. Conduct

A table is an argument about a claim, not about a person. Challenge the
proposition, the stake, the milestone or the evidence as hard as you like.

**Not permitted:** harassment or abuse of participants, tables constructed to
target or expose an individual, claims about a named person's character or
conduct, or any use of the registry to build a case against somebody.

A table whose real subject is a person rather than a proposition will be
removed, and its removal noted.

---

## 7. What may not be staked

Beyond the Protocol's rules on valid stakes:

**Nothing illegal.** Nothing requiring anyone to break the law, breach a duty of
confidence, or act against a professional obligation.

**Nothing that puts a person at risk.** Physical risk, safety, or welfare.

**Nothing belonging to someone who has not agreed.** See §3.

**No money on outcomes.** The Protocol's founding constraint. This registry
holds no funds, operates no escrow, and settles nothing in cash.

---

## 8. Withdrawal

**Before a table opens:** the creator may abandon it entirely. Nothing is
recorded.

**After it opens:** the table stands. A creator may stop working on it, and the
result will reflect what actually happened — but the table is not deleted,
because a registry that lets people erase inconvenient tables is worth nothing.

**A contributor may withdraw at any time.** Their name comes off. Their
commitment is recorded as withdrawn rather than completed, which is the honest
account of what occurred.
